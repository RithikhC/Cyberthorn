How many charging stations must be compromised?

An attacker has to push the grid past its spinning reserve before frequency collapses into load-shedding. We sweep the fraction of compromised chargers against a single-area frequency-response model (inertia, governor droop, reserve, under-frequency load shedding) and count only chargers that are actually plugged in with a car that will accept more current.

GridGuard architecture

We do not try to stop every exploit — chargers will be hacked. GridGuard assumes compromise and protects the grid physically: three independent detection layers feed a staged mitigation that throttles abnormal charging before the feeder relay or the under-frequency scheme trips.

1 · Physical layer (trusts nothing)

Feeder-level meter and charger-group sub-meter: ramp rate, CUSUM drift against a slow forecast, projected overload. Independent of the charger firmware and the backend, so it still works if both are owned.

2 · Cyber layer

Compares each charger's requested power with the schedule the charging-station management system (CSMS) authorised, plus a synchrony index: how many chargers stepped up within the same few seconds. Benign tariff surges pass; attacks do not.

3 · Coordination layer

A national coordinator correlates feeder alerts and PMU frequency / rate-of-change. If several feeders trip into THROTTLE together, the rest are pre-empted — attacks on many emirates at once are stopped before they sum to a grid event.

Hardware prototype (next phase)

  • ESP32 gateway in series with the charger: current transformer + voltage sense
  • Independent J1772/IEC 61851 pilot-PWM limiter and contactor — firmware on the charger cannot override it
  • Signed command channel and a local fail-safe: if the gateway loses contact it clamps to a safe default rate
  • The software here is the control logic and test-bed that the gateway firmware will mirror
